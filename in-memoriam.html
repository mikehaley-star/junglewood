In Memoriam

Honoring the beloved family members who shaped the Junglewood story

William Baker "Billy" Haley

June 24, 1941 – February 16, 2026

William Baker Haley was born June 24, 1941 in Indianapolis, Indiana to Ora Ben Haley, Jr. and Helen Baker Haley. He grew up at Junglewood in Lakewood, Colorado, the place that would become the backdrop for so many of the family's most treasured stories.

Bill attended Regis High School in Denver before going on to graduate from the University of Notre Dame. He earned his law degree from the University of Chicago Law School, setting the stage for a distinguished career devoted to justice and public service.

In his early career, Bill worked in Tennessee and New York in civil rights and legal services. He devoted his professional life to public-interest law and nonprofit work in New York, including roles with Legal Services NYC and the Single Parent Resource Center, where he collaborated with community organizers on tenant advocacy and contributed to Meals on Wheels.

Bill served as amicus curiae counsel in the landmark Supreme Court case Smith v. Organization of Foster Families for Equality & Reform, and appeared in several other significant cases including McMillan v. Board of Education and cases challenging the Rockefeller drug laws.

Bill & Christine

Bill and Christine Haley

Bill met his wife Christine Chabrol, a Parisian, through what began as a reluctant blind date in Chicago. What started with reluctance blossomed into a lifelong love story. Together they built a family and a life filled with purpose, warmth, and devotion.

Family

Bill is survived by his beloved wife Christine, their son John, and daughter Francesca, along with grandchildren Sophia, Nicolas, Marco, Poppy, and Aria. He is also survived by four brothers and two sisters.

He was preceded in death by his parents, two brothers, and notably his brother Danny, who passed away just five days after Bill.

In the Junglewood Tales

Billy's name echoes through the Junglewood stories — his steers and the infamous bulldog incident, the car adventures with Tommy and Dick, and the warmth he brought to everything he touched. As Benj wrote: "Billy — I believe you were more upset than anyone else was about this, as they were 'your' steers!" His presence runs deep through the fabric of the family's shared memory.

Tom Haley

Tommy's stories live on in the Junglewood Tales, woven throughout our family's shared memories. In the tales he comes alive as the spirited character known as Roy Rogers, riding the old red leather chair into the sunset. He composed "The Disbudding Blues," sang it with his guitar, and gave voice to the shared experience of greenhouse work that united the family.

Tommy remembered details others had forgotten — Harry the gardener, Albert and Shorty, the chicken dispatching that terrified young watchers, and the '41 Cadillac's adventures on those one-lane mountain roads. His contributions to preserving our history remain a testament to his importance in the Junglewood story.

Danny Haley

Passed February 2026, five days after his brother Bill

Danny was the heart and soul of the Junglewood Tales, contributing more stories than anyone else — 19 entries spanning all nine chapters. His vivid recollections, quick wit, and gift for storytelling brought Junglewood to life for readers who never walked its grounds.

From the legendary water tower painting caper to buying Uncle John's $5 Ford station wagon to the false teeth in the glove box, Danny turned everyday moments into unforgettable narratives. He had a special talent for drawing others into the conversation — prompting Tim to tell the sand-in-the-toilet story, coaxing Jim to share the smoking incident, and challenging his brothers to remember details they thought they'd forgotten.

Danny purchased his first car from Uncle John for $5 and a bicycle pump, found a $10 bill in the back seat, and returned false teeth with the classic reply: "Nope, they go with the car." He rode Shorty the horse through the willows at full gallop, stretched rubber bands across the house on Christmas Day, and once ate an entire package of Oreos in one sitting.

His love for his family shone through every story he told. As he wrote to his nieces: "If you or Kim ever come to Denver and would like to have a milk and cookie break for ol' times sake, I'd do it in a second! The memories were just too good."

Danny's stories live on throughout the Junglewood Tales, particularly in Chapters 1 through 9 — every single chapter bears his voice.
