The Tower Story

The water tower with "JUNGLEWOOD" painted in red — best viewed from Wadsworth

Southwest of Junglewood stands a monument to youthful ambition and daring—a silver water tower, over two hundred feet tall, that has stood sentinel over the landscape for decades. But it wasn't always an anonymous landmark. Once, during a single summer night in 1967, it became a canvas for an audacious declaration.

Dan and Marshal undertook what they would later describe as their own "Mission Impossible." Their mission: to paint "JUNGLEWOOD" across the tower's side in letters fifteen feet wide. They planned every detail with precision. They crafted a stencil, timing their move to slip through the gaps in the guard patrols. They arrived under cover of darkness with red paint and determination.

In just five minutes, under the stars and against the clock, they executed their plan flawlessly. The red paint announced their home to anyone who cared to look. The tower, once a neutral sentinel, had become a billboard for Junglewood, a wild and improbable proof that this place, these people, and their spirit mattered enough to mark forever.

The story lived on far longer than the paint. And that's the thing about Junglewood—the real monuments aren't built in steel or stone. They're built in the stories we tell, the risks we take, and the mark we leave on the world around us.
