The Patriarch

Ora Haley

December 4, 1845 – December 28, 1919 · From Maine to the American West

Ora Haley

Born December 4, 1845, East Corinth, Maine · Died December 28, 1919, Denver, Colorado

Son of Benjamin Haley and Nancy J. (Rollins) Haley · Married Augusta M. Pfeiffer on January 8, 1872 in Omaha, Nebraska · Father of Adelaide J. Haley, Anne G. (Haley) George, Martha Blanche (Haley) Smith, and Ora Benjamin Haley

A Young Man Headed West

Ora Haley was born on December 4, 1845 in East Corinth, a small town just under twenty miles outside of Bangor, Maine. The son of Benjamin Haley and Nancy J. Rollins, he was ambitious and independent from an early age. At nineteen, in 1864, he enlisted in the 1st Regiment, Maine Militia State Guards, Company A, serving for three months stationed at Fort McClary. After completing his service, he headed to Iowa, where he found life as a bullwhacker to his liking. In 1865, he drove a bull team west to Denver, Colorado — staying on in Denver and setting the course for an extraordinary life on the American frontier.

Building an Empire

By 1868, Ora had settled in Wyoming and was already helping shape the political landscape of the territory. He moved to Laramie, where he opened a successful butcher shop and quickly developed a keen understanding of the cattle trade. His business instincts were exceptional. In 1880, he founded the Two Bar brand, and over the following decade built the Two Bar Ranch into one of the largest individual cattle operations in Wyoming and western Colorado. At its peak, the operation encompassed over 36,000 acres in Wyoming with additional holdings along the Little Snake River in Colorado's Routt and Moffat Counties, running herds of 12,000 to 15,000 head of cattle.

Ora's vision extended beyond the range. He established the Haley Livestock and Trading Company, a sophisticated operation that by 1910 held over 10,000 livestock in Moffat County alone. The Two Bar Ranch became so significant to the history of the American West that its remaining structures in Brown's Park were later recognized by History Colorado as an important cultural landmark.

Public Service

Ora Haley was not only a cattleman but a civic leader. He was elected to the 2nd Legislative Assembly of the House of Representatives for the Territory of Wyoming in 1869, and later served in the first Wyoming State Senate from November 12, 1890 through January 10, 1891. A firm Republican throughout his life, his willingness to serve in public office reflected his deep commitment to the communities where he lived and worked, and his belief in building strong institutions for the frontier territories that were rapidly becoming states.

The Denver Years and Family Legacy

In 1913, after decades of building one of the West's great cattle empires, Ora sold his range holdings and retired to the Haley House in Denver, Colorado. It was in Denver that the Haley family would put down deep roots, eventually establishing Junglewood — the beloved family property with its nine greenhouses, open fields, and the spirit of togetherness that would become the setting for generations of cherished memories.

On January 8, 1872, Ora married Augusta M. Pfeiffer in Omaha, Nebraska. Together they had four children: Adelaide, Anne, Martha Blanche, and Ora Benjamin Haley. It was through this family line that Junglewood would eventually come to life.

Ora Haley passed away on December 28, 1919 in Denver, but the legacy he built lived on through his children, grandchildren, and the sprawling family that would one day gather around kitchen tables and email threads to share the stories that became the Junglewood Tales. His pioneering spirit, his willingness to take risks, and his deep love of the Western landscape are woven into every story on this site.

From a young man driving a bull team across the plains to a patriarch whose name still echoes through the family — Ora Haley's life was an extraordinary American story, and Junglewood is its lasting chapter.
